### Gaps and open questions

1. **The FY2005 PEP and equity-partner columns are the main gap.** S1/S2 give rank, revenue, lawyers and RPL for all 22 firms, but no PEP or equity-partner counts. Sourced or derived FY2005 PEP exists for only six firms: Wachtell, Cravath, Weil, Shearman, Cleary, and Skadden (approximate). The other 16 PEPs are [A] estimates, constrained by S3 (only ten firms above $2M; Cravath second at $2.6M). FY2005 equity partners were not found for any firm. To close this, read the Am Law 100 2006 PEP table: The American Lawyer, May 2006, via law.com subscription or an ALM Legal Intelligence download. If allowed, a Wayback copy of law.com's 2006 charts would also work.
2. **The path is thin.** FY2012 is the only year with PEP and equity partners for all 22 (S14). FY2007, FY2009 and FY2015 have revenue for a few firms only. FY2011 and FY2013 are not found for any firm. The pin needs a full Am Law 100 table for 2008, 2010, 2012, 2014 and 2016 (covering FY2007/09/11/13/15). I suggest a follow-up session with more search budget and law.com or Wayback access.
3. **FY2005 office counts are mostly [A].** They are inferred from today's counts on Wikipedia, plus dated openings and closures.
4. **Compensation systems are mostly [A].** They come from general industry knowledge. Only Debevoise (lockstep, single tier until 2025), Davis Polk (pure lockstep, all-equity), Baker (verein with regional pools) and Kirkland (two-tier, 2022 counts) were confirmed this session.
5. **Legal Week firm-reported PEP is not Am Law PEP.** Weil's $1.85M and Shearman's $1.48M are firms' own announcements as reported by Legal Week. Am Law's audited figures may differ: Cleary's revenue is $765M in Legal Week against $760M in Am Law. Prefer Am Law when it is available.
6. **Fiscal years.** Baker & McKenzie's fiscal year ended 30 June [A], so its "FY2005" is July 2004–June 2005. The game should decide whether to shift it.
7. **Fit with spec §10.1:** the spec's FY2009 "scale check" (Latham $1.82B, Jones Day $1.52B, Kirkland $1.43B) was not independently verified here. Baker's $2.112B and Skadden's $2.1B match S12.
8. **Personality fit.** Shearman, Fried Frank and Milbank fit `elite_lockstep` weakly. Skadden could be either `performance_machine` or `elite_lockstep`. White & Case is not a verein but behaves like `global_verein`. Reece may want an `elite_global` variant, or to accept these approximations.
9. **PT-0013 cautions.** Several dated events involve firm-level litigation or sanctions: the Sidley tax shelters and EEOC case, Mayer Brown/Refco, Cleary's 2007 sanctions, and Jones Day's Trump fees. They must stay firm-level events and must never attach to a `real`-flagged person.
10. **Beyond the window, unverified:** Wikipedia reports a November 2026 wind-down vote at Weil, Gotshal & Manges. This is after 2025 and outside the pin, but it should be confirmed before any later use.
