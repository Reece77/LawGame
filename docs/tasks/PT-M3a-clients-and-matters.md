# PT-M3a: Clients and matters (rules and API)

**Goal:** the heart of the game. Legal demand by practice flows to clients; clients instruct the firms on their panels; the firm checks conflicts, staffs named matters and flow work, and its people's hours drive progress, outcomes, relationships, utilisation, strain and learning.

**Spec:** §6, §7.1–7.6, §7.8 · **ADRs:** PT-0004, PT-0005, PT-0009, PT-0011 §1 (task mixes only; no eras) · **Streams:** `demand`, `intake`, `matter`, `conflict`, `client_gen` · **Depends on:** PT-M1b, PT-M2 · **Lane:** A

## Out of scope

Billing and money (PT-M3b: until it merges, hours post to WIP only); rivals beyond static 2005 panel firms with fixed appeal (PT-M5); screens beyond API and CLI (PT-M3c); AI eras (v2).

## Sub-tasks (commit after each)

1. **Practices data** for v1's ten practices (spec §6): demand base by region (one region in v1: the home market, sized from the dossier), drivers and sensitivities, rate cards by level, leverage and margin norms, matter kinds, task mixes, conflict density, cross-sell affinities. A short dossier `docs/research/v1-market.md` with sources.
2. **Clients:** 300 real clients (2005) and generated ones (`client_gen`), wallets by practice, panels (the player's firm on panels where the founders' books came from), GCs as people, relationships (spec §7.2) with relationship partners and origination credit.
3. **Demand and intake** (spec §6 formula, §7.4): monthly demand, allocation to clients, wallet shares by the appeal model, flow streams and named matters (ADR PT-0004 thresholds).
4. **Conflicts** (ADR PT-0009): parties, the index, refusals, waivers (an action), skipped checks' hidden conflicts. Property test: no matter is ever open adverse to a current client without a waiver.
5. **Staffing and work:** staffing actions (assign, set hours, remove), the default staffing rule (used by delegates and scripts), capacity and overtime, flow absorption; each person's monthly hours by matter and task; feeds growth, familiarity and strain (PT-M1a) and the hours morale term (PT-M1b).
6. **Progress and outcomes** (spec §7.5–7.6): phases, team output, supervision span on matters, deadlines and slips, litigation `p_win` with hidden merits, deal closing, errors (recorded; claims arrive in v2), settlement offers as pending items.
6a. **Courts and judges** (spec §7.6a): courts data, generated judges with procedural traits, random assignment, the judge's effect on duration, cost, settlement and sanctions (never on the side), forum choice, appearance familiarity. **Test:** swapping a case's judge never changes `p_win`'s sign of advantage; only path, timing and cost move.
7. **Relationships move** with outcomes, service and cross-practice breadth; cross-sell introductions (an action).
8. **API and CLI:** matters, clients, practices endpoints (revealed ranges only: merits and GC hidden traits never shown); `ptk matters`, `ptk staff`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Same seed, same intake and outcomes (golden).
- [ ] Hours reconcile: Σ person-hours = Σ matter-hours + flow hours, every month (property test).
- [ ] A stronger team (mean effective skill +3) wins at least 15 points more litigation on the same cases (printed over 1,000 seeded cases).
- [ ] An over-strained team (strain ≥ 80) makes at least twice the errors of a rested one.
- [ ] Named matters ≤ 40 open for a 50-lawyer firm (median), so the player's list stays manageable.
- [ ] `make check` green; `Milestone-Tag: pt-m3a-done`.

## Decisions made

Recorded by the PT-M3a session (2026-11-20). Numbers are `balance.yaml` defaults unless stated.

1. **Demand base.** The ten v1 practices carry the dossier's New York large-firm base of **$12.35bn** a year (practices.yaml order, the dossier's §4 central values); the other 12% ($1.65bn) is unserved until PT-M12 adds those practices (dossier §4 review). Spec §6's `ε ~ LogNormal(0, σ_p)` is drawn per practice a month (stream `demand`, key `(0, practice_index)`); `G` and `I_p` are 1.0 until PT-M7, so `β_p` is data now and takes effect then. Seasonality: deal practices ×1.22 in December, ×0.98 otherwise [A].
2. **Rate cards.** The §5 card (low/median/high by band) is firm-wide data. Each practice's partner rate is the §5 comparison table's dossier-view midpoint [D/A] (e.g. M&A $725, restructuring $800, employment $525); its other bands are the §5 medians × partner ÷ $700 [D], to the nearest $5, in integer cents. Levels map to bands as the dossier does (junior = years 1–2, mid = 3–5, senior = 6–8). The player's firm bills at its practice's card in PT-M3a, so the appeal's price term is 0 until rates can be set.
3. **Wallets.** A client's weight in practice p = revenue_bn^0.7 × sector intensity × the sector's practice mix (`clients.yaml`, [A]); each month's demand is split over all 1,000 clients (300 real, 700 generated) by weight, so wallets always sum to the market. The API shows a wallet *estimate* (±25%, $100k steps), never the true wallet.
4. **Panels.** Until PT-M5, a client's other panel firms are 16 static 2005 firms (`clients.yaml` `panel_firms`, keys from `docs/research/rivals-2005.md`) with a fixed appeal: base logit plus 0.5 in their strong practices. Large clients have 5–7 on the panel, others 3–5. The player's firm is on the panels its founding partners' books came from; new panels come with PT-M5's RFPs, new practices with cross-selling.
5. **Founders' books.** For each founding partner with a book (stream `book`, new id 34): clients are drawn by their expected fees to the firm in the partner's background practices, none above 50% of the book, until the expected fees reach the book (at most 12). The partner is relationship partner; relationships start at 55–70. A year of play earns 35–150% of the books in the tests (staffing and attrition decide where).
6. **Reputation stand-in.** The appeal's `Rep_{f,p}` is the firm's best partner's familiarity in the practice, (f − 1) / 19, until reputation (spec §12) arrives. Capacity term: minus last month's unserved share of the client's flow. Conflict term: 1 while the firm is adverse to the client.
7. **Intake (ADR PT-0004).** A practice's `flow_share` of the firm's fees is flow. The rest arrives as discrete matters of the practice's kinds with LogNormal sizes; only those at or above the named threshold are drawn individually (a Poisson count by inversion and a size from the LogNormal's tail); the smaller ones' expected fees join the flow. **Key clients** (top 20 by trailing-12-month fees): their matters are named from **$200,000** (`matters.key_named_min_cents`; tuned from $100,000, which gave medians of 41–44 open named matters at 50 lawyers, to 25–36). So routine advice for key clients stays flow; ADR PT-0004's other triggers are kept ($250,000; critical risk is drawn for named matters; the player's flag field exists for PT-M3c). Matter durations: the kind's range × the court's pace (× the judge's speed).
8. **Conflicts (ADR PT-0009).** Current clients: on the panel and instructed within 12 months; former within 36 (indexed only). A direct conflict either way (the adverse party is a current client, or the client is adverse on one of our open matters) holds the instruction as `conflicted` for one month: one waiver request (σ(−0.5 + 3 (R − 50)/50 + 0.15 (GC adaptability + risk appetite − 20)/2), −1.5 for an advance waiver; −2 relationship either way) opens it or declines it; unanswered, it is declined (−3). A client we are adverse to sends no work while that matter is open. With checks off, a conflicted matter opens unseen (`hidden_conflict`) and surfaces 3% a month: disqualified, −15 to both clients. Positional conflicts: −1 to each large same-sector key client when the firm opens a named matter for a large competitor.
9. **Staffing.** The default rule (module docstring of `ptk/sim/matters/staffing.py`) never plans anyone above their capacity; what it cannot place slips. Matters the player has not staffed are re-planned by it every month (`auto_staff`, the practice leader's job) until the player changes their team; `default_staffing` hands one back. Player staffing may plan up to 1.35 × capacity on named matters (overtime; `OverCapacityError` beyond). Flow fills up to 0.9 × capacity, home practice first, then anyone with familiarity 8+. Partners spend 25 hours a month on business development and management (non-billable, counted for strain).
10. **Tasks and learning.** People do the tasks of a phase within one task level of their own (else the nearest); hours on tasks at or one level above their own are substantive (learn(h), ADR PT-0006 §4). A matter's hours are its expected fees ÷ the kind's blended rate; a phase needs its share of them at effective skill 10, so weaker or strained teams take longer.
11. **Outcomes (spec §7.6).** Cases: `p_win = σ(0.3 (Q_us − Q_them) + m)`, merits `m ~ N(0, 1)` and opposing counsel `~ N(12, 2)` drawn at intake (hidden); `Q_us` = hours-weighted craft (advocacy and legal reasoning 0.35 each, writing and procedure 0.15) + 0.5 per 10 of the lead's big moments over 10 − 3 × the preparation shortfall; a case goes to trial six months after its due month whatever its progress. Deals and restructurings: `p_close = σ(1.5 + 0.25 (Q − 10) − 0.5 if critical)`. Advisory and filings: quality only (14+ counts as a win for the relationship, 8 or less as a loss). Over 1,000 seeded cases effective skill 10 wins 40.4%, 13 wins 57.7%.
12. **Errors.** 2% a matter-month at skill 10, rested, supervised; × (1 + strain/100) hours-weighted, × 2 in burnout's effects, × 2 for a cowboy, × (1 + supervision shortfall). 70% × the supervision factor are caught (3% rework hours); escaped errors are recorded, hidden from the API until claims arrive (v2). Over 20,000 matter-months: 398 rested, 1,402 at strain 85.
13. **Courts and judges (spec §7.6a).** 49 generated judges in ten forums (stream `judge_gen`, new id 33), traits 1–20 from N(10, 3.5). A judge is assigned at a case's first month (stream `matter`) and moves duration (docket speed), discovery hours (strictness), settlement offers (pressure, trial appetite), summary judgment (motions end), sanctions on escaped errors and the value of procedure skill and appearance familiarity on progress; `p_win` has no judge argument (tested). Forum choice: in the intake month, among the kind's forums.
14. **Settlements.** Offers arrive 4% a month in discovery and motions (× the judge's term), at the other side's noisy view of our expected recovery × 0.85, open for a month; accepting moves the relationship by 10 × (offer − the true expected recovery).
15. **Relationships (spec §7.2).** Monthly: drift 2% toward 40 (halved at loyalty 20), −2 × unserved flow share (tuned from −6, which collapsed founders' relationships to 0 within a year when a small firm was over capacity), −2 if the client's team averages strain over 60, +0.6 per 10 of the relationship partner's client care over 10, +0.3 per extra practice (cap 1.2); outcomes ±8 (critical ×2). Origination credit: each hour's standard value goes to the matter's originator (flow: the relationship partner), by calendar year.
16. **General counsel** are a `GeneralCounsel` on the client (name, age, hidden personality), not employed `Person`s: they have no skills in v1. GC moves and alumni (spec §7.3) are later work.
17. **Storage.** Schema v5 keeps key columns plus a JSON payload per client, relationship and matter; clients and judges are written once; the month's hours are appended to `hours_log`.
18. **Acceptance harness.** The 50-lawyer test grows the group-lift firm to 50 lawyers on panels worth about $750k a lawyer and replaces each leaver (PT-M1b's ~18% attrition would otherwise shrink an un-hiring firm); it counts open named matters for months 12–48. Medians, seeds 1–5: 28, 36, 25, 25, 34.5.

## Follow-ups

- **PT-M3b:** post WIP from `hours_log.value_cents` (already rounded once per person, matter and month); write-offs for overruns and over-staffing (matters record `errors_caught` rework and over-budget hours).
- **PT-M5:** replace the static panel firms with acting rivals, and RFPs as the way onto new panels (in PT-M3a the firm can only cross-sell into clients it already has).
- **Early case assessment** (spec §7.6: merits revealed as a range) is not built; PT-M3c's "win probability as a band" needs it or a band from the team's craft.
- **Default staffing plans to budget hours at skill 10,** so typical teams (effective skill 8–9) overrun: closed matters took about 11 months against 7 planned in the 50-lawyer test. PT-M7 may want the rule to plan for the team's expected output, or budgets to assume an average team.
- **Attrition without hiring** shrinks every scenario firm (PT-M1b's resignations now see real hours); a default campus or lateral plan, or delegates, would keep a passive game steady.
- **Pro bono (spec §7.8)** is listed in the brief's spec line but not in its sub-tasks; not built.
- **Clients' lives** (born, grow, merge, fail; spec §7.1), **GC moves and alumni** (spec §7.3), **outside counsel guidelines** and **PE sponsors' portfolio companies** are not built.
- **Rates.** The player's firm bills at the card; a rate-setting action (and the appeal's price term) belongs with PT-M7's yearly rate rises.
- **Turn time.** A 360-turn golden game now takes about 15 s through the database (clients are memoised; relationships and matters are rewritten each turn). Writing only changed matters would halve it if PT-M7's budgets need it.
