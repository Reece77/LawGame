# PT-M13: Mergers and the verein

**Goal:** firms combine. The firm can approach any firm, a real rival or a generated one, and be approached. Due diligence narrows what the firm knows of the other side's books, partners, conflicts, annuities and leases. Terms set the points ratio, the name, the managing partner and governance, and the harmonisation of comp systems and rates. Both partnerships vote, each by two thirds. Every client of both firms clears conflicts, and conflicted work is lost. Integration then tests the combination: culture clash, partners whose PEP falls leave, low-rate clients balk at the merged card. A verein instead joins member firms under one brand while each keeps its own books and profit pool, so a firm can grow abroad without pooling profit. Accepting a merger in which the firm is absorbed ends the campaign as a scored exit.

**Spec:** §8.8 (mergers and the verein), §8.5 (comp systems), §8.7 (flight and the spiral), §10.1-§10.3 (rivals and history's mergers), §11.2 (conflicts), §12.2 (merger press) · **ADRs:** PT-0003 (partnership accounting), PT-0007 (governance and votes), PT-0008 (rivals at real scale, pinned to history), PT-0009 (conflicts), PT-0010 (ownership by jurisdiction), PT-0012, PT-0013 (real people) · **TST to port:** formulas.md §28 (mergers and acquisitions: diligence, offers, integration and the exit, `tst/sim/mna/`) and §31 (the group: member companies with their own books, `tst/sim/group/`), re-cast for partnerships · **Depends on:** PT-M7c (`pt-m7c-done`); uses PT-M11's offices and London · **Lane:** B (beside PT-M12 in lane A and PT-M15 in lane C)

## Sub-tasks (commit after each)

1. **Research dossier** `docs/research/law-firm-mergers.md` (sources, S/D/A): large-firm mergers 2005-2025 (deal counts by year from the Altman Weil and Fairfax trackers, the significant combinations already in `firms.yaml`'s history), how terms were set (points ratios, name, governance, comp harmonisation), partner losses in the two years after a merger, client losses from conflicts, rate harmonisation, failed mergers and votes that failed, and the verein: which firms used one (Baker McKenzie, DLA Piper, Dentons, Norton Rose Fulbright, Hogan Lovells, Squire Patton Boggs, King & Wood Mallesons) and how costs and profits were shared. **ADR PT-0013:** firm-level facts only; no misconduct or blame attached to a real person, including in failed combinations.
2. **Targets and approaches:** a target list with a fit view (practices, offices, rates, PEP, culture from comp system and personalities); an approach costs the MP's energy and a confidentiality window; rivals approach the firm by a utility rule (complementary practices, a weaker PEP, a new region). Before 2026 rivals merge with each other only as history did (PT-0008); after 2025, by the seeded world's rules (PT-0015).
3. **Due diligence** (TST §28.2): costs professional fees and months; reveals the other firm's books exactly and its partners' quality, annuity burden, leases and conflict overlap as ranges that narrow with effort. Hidden true values never reach the API or a prompt (test).
4. **Terms and votes:** the points ratio (each firm's partners' share of the merged profit pool), the name, the MP and executive committee, the comp system and rate harmonisation; the rival's partnership answers by its partners' expected PEP change and personalities; each side's partners vote by PT-0007's rules with a two-thirds threshold. Counter-terms and walking away.
5. **Conflicts clearance and closing:** every client of both firms checked (PT-0009); conflicted matters are resolved at closing (the firm keeps the better relationship, the other client leaves, waivers where possible). Closing combines the books: capital accounts carried, receivables and WIP carried, leases and annuities assumed, the client books combined exactly. Accounts and the merged firm's identity are ADR PT-0016's.
6. **Integration** (TST §28.5): culture clash from differing comp systems and personalities; partners whose PEP falls, or whose practice is subordinated, leave at a raised rate through PT-M4's flight machinery (the spiral can follow); low-rate clients facing the merged card leave at a rate; office consolidation; press moments.
7. **The verein:** member firms keep their own books, partners and profit pools under a shared brand; shared costs (brand, technology, management) by a formula of each member's revenue; reputation and referrals shared; conflicts checked across the verein. A London member firm is a way to open London (PT-M11's follow-up, "local mergers as a way to open a city"). No firm entry spans two members.
8. **Being absorbed and the exit:** a larger firm's approach the player accepts, which both partnerships pass, ends the campaign as an exit scored by the founder's capital, PEP history and the merged firm's standing; a merger of equals keeps the campaign going with the player as MP or not, by the terms.
9. **Strategies and readings:** register a `merger` strategy (steady that merges with a complementary mid-size firm in 2010-2012) and a `verein` strategy (steady that joins a London member firm); read them against steady with lane A.
10. **Screens:** Mergers (targets, diligence, terms, the two votes, clearance, integration's progress), the verein on the Firm screen, the exit ending; e2e: merge with a generated firm through both votes and clearance, and form a verein with a London firm.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At closing, the merged firm's balance sheet equals the two firms' balance sheets combined less the eliminations ADR PT-0016 defines, and the client book is combined exactly (property test); every entry balances.
- [ ] After clearance, no matter of the merged firm is adverse to one of its clients without a waiver (property test).
- [ ] A merger passes only with two thirds of each partnership (deterministic tests); a rival's answer reads its partners' expected PEP change.
- [ ] In a verein, no entry spans two member firms and each member's PEP comes from its own pool (property test).
- [ ] Integration losses are realistic: partners lost within 24 months of closing in the dossier's band (printed on seeds 1-10; the band in `balance-targets.md` as T13, proposed by the session for the product manager's approval).
- [ ] Rivals not merged with the firm hold T11 and T11.2025 (seeds 1-10); a rival merged with the firm leaves its path without failing T11.
- [ ] The `merger` strategy's lawyers and revenue in 2015 against steady printed on seeds 1-10 and 11-40 (T3's growth is expected partly from here); T1-T12 re-read by lane A with no new miss without an authorised row.
- [ ] No hidden value reaches the API or a prompt; no real person is attached to misconduct or blame in merger events or press (tests).
- [ ] T12 (≤ 250 ms) holds at December 2025 with a merged firm and a verein member.
- [ ] `make check`, `make e2e` and the nightly run green; `Milestone-Tag: pt-m13-done`.

## Decisions made

ADR PT-0016 records the structural ones (books at closing, identity, the pin, the exit, streams 130-137). The rest, with their numbers (all `mergers.*` in `balance.yaml`, [A] unless tagged):

1. **Rivals are aggregates, so closing materialises them.** The other firm's key people join as they are (real notables keep their names; ADR PT-0013 holds: no merger log or press moment names or links a person), the rest of its pools are generated (stream `merger_people`). Integrated targets: at most `max_lawyers` 400 lawyers, headquartered in a modelled city; larger or elsewhere: verein only.
2. **Absorption is by identity** (spec 8.8 read with "a merger of equals keeps the campaign going with the player as MP or not, by the terms"): the player's firm is absorbed when it takes the other firm's name *and* MP, or brings under `absorbed_below_bp` 1,000 (10%) of the pool. A first draft used a 30% share line; in play almost every mid-size target was then an "absorption" for a 3-15 partner firm, which the spec's merger of equals rules out. Exit is a new `GameOutcome` (extends ADR PT-0007 4; not a defeat).
3. **The points ratio** sets the incoming equity partners' lockstep points (their side's total = ours x (1 - s) / s) and their first budgets (their share of the expected pool); formula systems pay by their formula from the first December. Both partnerships expect `answer.synergy_bp` 800 (8%) on the pool (the case mergers are sold on; dossier s3 notes two thirds of large mergers then grew PEP more slowly).
4. **Two thirds** is `vote_bp` 6,667 of votes cast on each side, PT-M4's convention for its two-thirds votes (7 of 9 passes, 6 of 9 does not). The other partnership is counted partner by partner (at most 60 drawn) on its expected PEP change and the terms (MP 0.25, name 0.15, comp 0.2, card rise 1.0 a unit), with one counter a round and `max_rounds` 2.
5. **The book** is drawn when talks open (a rival's approach draws nothing until the player opens talks, so a firm that never deals plays exactly as before): panel clients first, then the city's market by wallet in the firm's practices, until the merged firm's expected work (wallet x its logit share) reaches `book.coverage` 1.0 x the other firm's revenue, practice by practice in its lawyers' mix, at most 80 clients, one practice each, scores 45-70, 6% with an adverse engagement. Earlier drafts ($750k of revenue per client, two practices) gave a healthy firm but 1.0 s turns; $3M per client gave fast turns and a firm that collapsed (85% of partners gone): the expected-work rule holds both (T12 158 ms; T13 in band).
6. **Diligence:** levels 1-3, each 25 bp of the other firm's revenue (at least $150k, to 6600) and 2 months (the report arrives in the second); half widths 0.6/0.35/0.18/0.08; books exact from level 1 (revenue, profit, PEP, WIP, receivables, cash, revolver), capital never shown (it would reveal the annuities).
7. **The closing sheet** (ADR PT-0016 2): WIP 60 and receivables 75 days of fees (lockup 135, T7's band), cash a month of costs, revolver 25% of working capital, annuities 0-35% of it (hidden), lease 2-10 years (hidden); the home-city lease is surrendered (6 months' rent) as the eliminated line; annuities carried as one record per deal (person id minus the deal id, shown as "Retired partners of ..."), paid over 10 years; the facility grows 1.5x the carried debt.
8. **Two fixes the first closings exposed** (both in the mergers module): carried WIP and receivables raise `wc_closed_cents`, so the December cash-basis profit is not cut by the working capital that came with the other firm's capital; and in December a merged partner's next budget is their points' share of the year (the allocation weights a part year, as PT-M7c found for December admissions).
9. **Integration** (24 months): flight x (1 + 0.8 x clash) for the other side, x 1.5 for a partner whose budget fell more than 10%, x 1.5 for one whose side holds under 25% of their practice; the other side's budgets at closing are guaranteed 12 months (a PT-M4 guarantee the partnership funds; dossier s3: harmonisation takes 0-24 months); clients balk at 120 bp a month per unit of card rise x sensitivity / 10 for 12 months. **Tuned for T13** (rule 1, my keys): `flight_mult` 1.2 -> 0.8, `pep_fall_mult` 1.5 -> 0.5 (+ tolerance 0.1), `subordinated_mult` 1.0 -> 0.5, `protection_months` 0 -> 24 -> 12 (24 dissolved seed 5's merged firm). T13 median: 69% (before the two fixes in 8) -> 28-29% -> 13.5% on seeds 1-10 (merged on 4: 16.7%, 0%, 23.9%, 10.3%).
10. **Clash** = the comp systems' distance (`clash_comp`) + 0.3 x the rate cards' gap (a 20% gap is the full term); a rival's comp system comes from its personality (firms.yaml's comp texts are [A] labels).
11. **The verein:** costs $250k a month + 20 bp of the members' monthly revenue, split by revenue [A: no source gives a formula, dossier s8]; a referral every 3 months (an introduction at 20-35); no reach penalty and +0.1 appeal per member in a member's city; conflicts across the verein through `conflict_index(shared=...)`; a member more than ten times the player's size loses 1.0 a tenfold beyond (Clifford Chance joining a four-lawyer start-up was the first reading).
12. **Rivals:** approach the player at 40 bp a month (at most once in 24 months, a firm of 20+ lawyers, utility at least 0.3); one bringing over 70% of the pool proposes absorption. From 2026, 6% a quarter that one rival merges into a buyer (ADR PT-0015's world); this changes golden runs after 2025 only (golden seed 1 is identical with these draws off).
13. **Talks:** confidential 6 months (a leak: a press moment, -3 confidence to the partners); lapse after 9 idle months; 12 months' cooldown; only the MP acts.
14. **T12** is read as its definition (a 150-lawyer firm, 2025's turns): the firm merged in January 2006 with the smallest generated New York firm and has a London verein member, 158 ms; a merger closed in January 2025 (180 lawyers) reads 239 ms (printed, nightly).
15. **Identical-result speed-ups in shared files** (CLAUDE.md 9 rule 6): billing's `post_wip` sums a month's records per line before posting, staffing computes a client's card once per practice, `MarketState.relationship` and `matter` index by the tuple's identity; golden seed 1 matched its old snapshot with the new draws off.
16. **"The Firm screen"** does not exist: the verein shows on the Overview (and the Mergers screen); the exit on both.
17. **Strategies** (`ptk/balance/mergers.py`, one line in `strategies.py`): `merger` approaches the most complementary integrable firm (at most 6x its lawyers, a fair share of at least 20%) in 2010-2012, one level of diligence, terms at the fair share with a combined name, its own MP and comp system, a blended card, accepts a counter of at least 20%; `verein` approaches the London firm most open to a verein from 2010.
18. **e2e** uses copies of the practice save (`POST /api/practice/{key}?slug=`, new) in January 2011 and PT-M4's test-only confidence route so the player's partners back the counter.
