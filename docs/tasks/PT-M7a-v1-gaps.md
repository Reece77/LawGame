# PT-M7a: v1 gaps

**Goal:** close the gaps Reece's product manager found reviewing PT-M3a to PT-M6 (2026-11-20) before PT-M7 balances v1: a reachable first partnership vote in the default scenario, default staffing that fits typical teams and fills idle capacity, pro bono, early case assessment, yearly rate rises with the appeal's price term, and Holiday or "continue until" that stops for what needs the player.

**Spec:** §4.2 (Holiday), §5.5, §7.1 (price term), §7.5, §7.6 (early case assessment), §7.8 (pro bono), §15 (critical events) · **ADRs:** TST 0005 (hidden values), PT-0004, PT-0005, PT-0006 §4 (learning), PT-0012 (streams), PT-0013 · **Targets:** `docs/balance-targets.md` T7 (utilisation), T10 (burnout) · **Follow-ups closed:** PT-M3a (staffing at skill 10, early case assessment, pro bono, rates), PT-M3c (win-probability band), PT-M6 (Holiday and critical events) · **Depends on:** PT-M3a–PT-M6 (all merged) · **Lane:** A (before PT-M7)

## Sub-tasks (commit after each)

1. **Scenario fix.** In `biglaw_spinout` the starting corporate associate is a fourth-year (`associate_4`, class 4 in January 2005), so at the default track of 8 years they are eligible at the December 2008 vote. A test plays the scenario to December 2008 and finds them eligible. Golden snapshots regenerated, the reason in `CHANGELOG.md`.
2. **Default staffing for expected output.** The default rule plans each member's hours from their expected output (effective skill on the kind's tasks ÷ 10 × the strain factor, floored), not from skill 10, so a typical team (effective skill 8–9) finishes near the planned months; a matter near its end takes the hours its remaining output needs at the team's expected output. Flow fills idle capacity before anyone is left unassigned: after the home and familiar pools, remaining idle fee earners take what is left of any practice's flow. Pro bono flow (sub-task 3) comes after paid work. Tunables in `balance.yaml`.
3. **Pro bono (spec §7.8).** Pro bono flow (a monthly pool of hours per lawyer) and named pro bono matters (occasional cases and advisory work for a pro bono client) with no fees: no WIP, no bills, no origination, no client relationship. The firm's **credit policy** (default 50 hours a year counting toward each lawyer's billable target; an action). Effects: associates' morale (a morale term for pro bono hours) and learning (pro bono hours count as substantive), and recruiting appeal (the firm's pro bono hours per lawyer in the offer score's prestige). Utilisation stays billable hours over capacity. A **Pro bono** column (year-to-date hours) on the People screen and a credit-policy setting on the Org screen.
4. **Early case assessment (spec §7.6).** An action on an open case (litigation, arbitration, investigation): energy from the actor and non-billable hours from the lead this month; it reveals the case's merits as a range whose width shrinks with the lead's legal reasoning and judgment (stream `actions`), always containing the true merits. PT-M3c's outlook band uses the range's midpoint when it exists, and the Explain item shows the range in words. Merits stay hidden in every API schema and every prompt (tests).
5. **Yearly rate rises.** Each January the firm's rate card (its rate index over the practices' 2005 cards) rises by a player-set percentage (default: the market's, `rivals.rate_inflation`); an action sets it. Every hour's standard value, blended engagements and new matters' expected fees use the indexed card. The appeal's **price term** (spec §7.1) is `1 − firm index ÷ market index`, so the default firm stays neutral and a firm that raises faster than the market loses share. Rivals already set their own rates (PT-M5). The rate rise setting and the current index on the Org screen.
6. **Holiday and "continue until"** (spec §4.2; PT-M6 follow-up): play up to N months, or until a date, each month its own transaction (ADR-0004), stopping before a month when: a critical event waits (never answered for the player), the next month is December and the partnership has candidates to propose or a vote open, a settlement offer is open, or a rival's approach to a partner is open; also at the game's end. The API returns the months played and the stop reason; a Holiday control in the header; `ptk holiday` in the CLI.

## Decisions made

Recorded by the PT-M7a session (2026-11-20/21). Numbers are `balance.yaml` defaults.

1. **The acceptance firm** is PT-M3a's 50-lawyer grown firm (`grown_firm`, leavers replaced), months 13-48, seeds 1-5; utilisation is the median of the seeds' medians, burnout the pooled rate of associates' burnout starts (`tests/balance/test_pt_m7a.py`). **Before:** utilisation medians 76.1, 64.3, 60.5, 68.0, 68.4% (median 68.0%), burnout 0-1%, closed matters about 2x planned (11 months against 6). **After:** 78.1, 77.4, 73.3, 80.8, 86.7% (median 78.1%), burnout 0.4% a year pooled, closed matters 1.00-1.33x planned (median 1.11).
2. **Why matters ran long.** Two causes: the rule planned hours at skill 10, and (a PT-M3a bug) a matter a hair from done rounded its remaining hours to zero, never finished, and kept its team's planned hours reserved, starving other matters. Fixed by rounding up (`math.ceil`) and planning a matter's last month only for its remaining work.
3. **Expected output** = effective skill on the *current phase's* tasks / 10 x the strain factor, held to 0.3-1.3 (`staffing.output_floor`, `output_cap`); the kind's whole mix underestimated hours on partner-heavy phases.
4. **Headroom.** The rule plans named work up to 95% of capacity (`staffing.plan_fill`; PT-M3a planned to 100%, which put one seed's burnout at 10.7% a year); `matters.flow_fill` 0.90 -> 0.95.
5. **Idle capacity.** Flow a practice's own and familiar people cannot take goes to anyone with free capacity before it is unserved. Named matters draw on other practices' people only with familiarity 8+ (`matters.flow_min_familiarity`): letting unfamiliar people staff them billed far over budget and took PT-M3b's realisation guard to 81.4% (band 84-92%); with the rule it is 84.0%.
6. **Budgets** (expected fees) stay at skill-10 hours: a typical team's extra hours are the existing overrun write-down's business. The PM's "budgets fit typical teams" is met as durations: matters now finish in their planned months.
7. **Pro bono client.** Pro bono work belongs to a sentinel client, id 0 (`PRO_BONO_CLIENT`), never in the client universe, with a database row so matters' foreign key holds. Its hours have no value; billing never sees them (`interfaces.month_hours`, `billing_matters`). Named pro bono matters: 50 bp a month per lawyer (one every four months at 50 lawyers), 150-600 hours over 4-12 months, four kinds (`pro_bono.named_kinds`). Flow pool: (12 + 1.0 x credit) / 12 hours a lawyer a month, from free capacity up to 90%.
8. **Pro bono effects.** Hours-term: credited pro bono counts toward the billable target (policy 0-300 h a year). Morale: +3 target points at 8 h a month (associates). Learning: every pro bono hour is substantive. Recruiting: +0.004 offer-score points per pro bono hour a lawyer a year (cap 0.4), in `prestige`. Reputation and the Beloved trait wait for v2.
9. **Early case assessment**: once per case, by the player, for a case with a lead; 6 energy, 20 lead hours this month (non-billable `other` hours). Half-width 1.2 logit points at assessment skill 10 (legal reasoning 0.6, advocacy 0.2, commercial awareness 0.2), 35% narrower per 10 points above, never below 0.25; the true merits sit within 60% of the half-width of the centre. The API and the inbox show the range in words; the outlook uses its midpoint.
10. **Rates.** Demand stays work valued at the 2005 cards until PT-M7's macro history; the firm's index scales every hour's value, blended rates and new matters' expected fees (hours from a matter's size are unchanged). The market's rise is `rivals.rate_inflation` (3%); the policy is -10% to +20%. Rate and pro bono settings are on the Org screen's Firm policies panel; the API under `/policies`.
11. **Holiday** is synchronous (TST's background job and "While you were away" summary are follow-ups). It stops after a month for: game over, a critical event, a vote (any open vote, or December with an eligible associate), a rival approach or a settlement offer made during the holiday, the date, the months (at most 36). It cannot start while a critical event waits (409).
12. **PT-M4's exodus guard** (`test_the_exodus_spiral`, at least 3 of 10 unmanaged firms dissolve after three partners leave) misses on this branch: 0 of 10 (main 4); seeds 11-40: 5 of 30 (main 9). The stalled-matter fix alone takes main to 2 of 10; the rest is idle capacity filled after the exodus. No PT-M7a key brings it back without undoing the brief. Reece (2026-11-21): merge; PT-M7 owns T9's spiral; the test is a strict xfail naming PT-M7, its assertion unchanged.
13. **The m5 e2e** passed on a race: approached partners were never on the Recruitment screen (they are lateral candidates on the Partnership screen); its check matched the rival page before navigation. Fixed to wait for the Partnership screen; the rival page's link now goes there.
