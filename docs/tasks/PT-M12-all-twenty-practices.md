# PT-M12: All twenty practices

**Goal:** the market has all twenty practices of spec §6, not v1's ten. International arbitration, tax, antitrust, financial regulation, healthcare and life sciences, energy and infrastructure, technology and privacy, private client, insurance and plaintiff mass torts each have their own demand driver and cycle, matter kinds, billing mix, rates, leverage and conflict density. Clients' wallets, rivals' practice mixes and cross-selling cover every pair. Plaintiff work runs on contingency, and from about 2010 litigation funders can carry its WIP. The firm can enter a new practice by a lateral hire or a team, and a specialist firm in a counter-cyclical or regulatory niche is a real way to play.

**Spec:** §6 (the table and demand), §7.1 (wallets), §7.2 (cross-selling), §7.7 (contingency, retainers, success fees), §14 (eras' policy events: Dodd-Frank 2010, the 2017 tax reform, GDPR 2018) · **ADRs:** PT-0004 (two tiers), PT-0008 (rivals at real scale, pinned), PT-0009 (conflicts), PT-0012, PT-0014 (macro history), PT-0015 (the seeded world) · **Depends on:** PT-M7c (`pt-m7c-done`); inherits the known misses T3 lawyers, T3 revenue and T4 steady (owner PT-M18) · **Lane:** A (beside PT-M13 in lane B and PT-M15 in lane C)

## Sub-tasks (commit after each)

1. **Research dossier** `docs/research/practices-v2.md` (sources, S/D/A, as v1's `v1-market.md`): for each of the ten new practices, the US large-firm market in 2005 (demand by practice, with the total market checked against v1's ten), partner and associate rates in New York, leverage, margin, realisation and collection norms, the matter kind mix, the billing model mix (retainers in private client, success fees in arbitration, contingency in mass torts), seasonality, conflict density, its driver series to 2025 with dated policy events (Sarbanes-Oxley's tail, Dodd-Frank in July 2010, the Affordable Care Act in 2010, the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, GDPR in May 2018 and the CCPA in 2020, the shale boom and the 2014-16 oil crash, the Inflation Reduction Act in 2022, catastrophe years for insurance), London's share for each, and which rivals were strong in each (Chambers bands by practice, as PT-M5's dossier did). Plaintiff mass torts: contingency shares, case durations, settlement sizes and the litigation-funding market from about 2010.
2. **Data** (`practices.yaml`): the ten new practices with spec §6's columns and every key the ten v1 practices carry, values from the dossier with their markers; `cross_sell` affinities for every pair of the twenty (symmetric where the dossier gives no reason otherwise). **Test:** a practice the firm and the market never touch changes nothing (seed 1's goldens before the new practices draw any demand are unchanged until the first month they are drawn; regenerate them once with the reason in CHANGELOG).
3. **Demand drivers:** new series in `macro_history.yaml` (to 2025 from the dossier) and their seeded paths to 2034 (`macro_seed`, PT-0015), with each practice's `beta`: arbitration rising, tax spiking with reform, antitrust following M&A with policy, financial regulation surging after 2010, privacy rising after 2018, energy on long cycles, private client on wealth, insurance on catastrophe and claims cycles, mass torts lumpy. **Test:** 2009 demand against 2008 falls for the pro-cyclical new practices and rises for the counter-cyclical ones, and Dodd-Frank, the 2017 reform and GDPR show in their practices' demand within a year (seeds 1-10).
4. **Clients and wallets:** real clients' wallets in the new practices (their sectors drive them: banks in financial regulation, insurers in insurance, energy companies in energy); generated clients get them from their sector and size; individuals are pooled flow demand for private client and mass torts by region (spec §7.1). London clients get the new practices by `cities.yaml`'s mix.
5. **Rivals:** each real rival's practice mix covers the twenty from the dossier, so that T11 (rivals' revenue pinned within ±15% to 2025) still holds with demand split over more practices. Rival practice specialists (an arbitration boutique, a private client firm, a plaintiff firm) where the dossier names them.
6. **Contingency and funding** (spec §7.7): plaintiff matters carry years of WIP with no bill and pay a share of the recovery (25-40%); from 2010 a litigation funder may fund a case's WIP for a share of the recovery (non-recourse: a loss costs the firm nothing more; a win pays the funder first). Accounts are your decision (an ADR if you add a liability account); client money from a recovery passes through the client book (`C` accounts) before the firm's fee.
7. **Entering a practice:** a firm opens a practice with a lateral partner (PT-M4's laterals), a lateral team or by a partner retraining (slow familiarity, PT-0006); a new practice group follows PT-M2's rules. The scenario's starting practices are unchanged.
8. **Strategies and readings:** register a specialist strategy (a counter-cyclical or regulatory niche: your choice from the dossier, for example restructuring and financial regulation) and a full-service strategy that enters a new practice each year from 2008; read T1-T12 with all twenty practices on seeds 1-10 and 11-40.
9. **Screens:** the Practices screen shows twenty (filters for driver and cycle, the firm's position in each, an Enter practice action); the Matter screen shows contingency and funding terms; e2e: enter international arbitration with a lateral partner and fund a mass-tort case.

## Decisions made

1. **Sizing (sub-task 1).** v1's ten stay as they are ($12.35bn); the ten new practices add $2.20bn, the top of v1's dossier range for "other" ($1.2-2.2bn), so the twenty total $14.55bn, +3.9% on v1's $14.0bn New York large-firm market (criterion 1's test reads it against that $14.0bn: the D0s are the New York region's, as v1's). Stopping at the range's top rather than a bottom-up $3bn+ allows for the tax, antitrust and regulatory hours already inside v1's M&A and litigation cut (`docs/research/practices-v2.md` s1).
2. **Appending without moving a draw (sub-task 2).** The ten practices, their lawyer roles and their macro series are appended. A person's familiarity with v1's ten is drawn from their own generator as before; the later practices draw from a jump of it (`practices.yaml familiarity_main_draws` 10, `np.random.Generator(PCG64.jumped())`), so no other draw moves. The seeded world's recession regime keeps its stream key (12, v1's `len(SERIES)`), and the appended indices take keys 13-22 (`macro_seed.series_key`; ADR PT-0012's table). The directory-bands inbox line lists the bands in practices the firm has lawyers in, and a tie for the press's top band takes the practice first in file order (it took the alphabetically first key, so an untouched "antitrust" outranked "banking_finance").
3. **The "changes nothing" test** (`tests/unit/test_pt_m12_practices.py`): seed 1's steady game with the v2 practices present but untouched (demand a cent a year, no mix, strength or notable naming them) is identical month by month to v1's ten alone (ledger balances and inbox every month; people, matters and balances at the end): 48 months in `check`, 132 (to December 2015) nightly. Goldens are regenerated once for the sub-task (CHANGELOG): every person carries twenty familiarities, v1's cross-sell pairs are completed (sub-task 2's "every pair": a v1 pair one side listed takes that value, one neither listed 0.1; any pair with private client or mass torts 0.05 unless the dossier gives one), and the seeded world's points carry the new series.
4. **Cross-sell (sub-task 2).** Every pair of the twenty, symmetric for every pair with a v2 practice (v2 s5, spec 6's "M&A with antitrust, tax, employment, finance"); v1's own asymmetric values are kept.
5. **Drivers (sub-task 3).** Ten new indices in `macro_history.yaml` (2005-2025, July points from the dossier's s4 table, and dated points for the 2008 crash, Dodd-Frank, the TCJA, GDPR and the IRA); betas arbitration 0.5, tax 0.8, antitrust 1.0, financial regulation 0.6, healthcare 0.5, energy 0.9, technology and privacy 0.6, private client 0.7, insurance 0.5, mass torts 0.6 (all [A], v2 s4), each reading an index that carries its own sign (as v1's restructuring reads `credit`). The seeded world fits them as it fits v1's; the rising ones (arbitration, healthcare, privacy, wealth) join `macro.seeded.trend_series` (they had reverted to their 2016-2025 mean, so wealth fell from 1.75 to 1.48 by 2034 on seed 1). Readings (seeds 1-10, 12 months on 12, every seed in the same direction): 2009 against 2008 tax -11.4%, antitrust -37.8%, energy -30.2%, technology and privacy -7.9%, private client -4.9%; arbitration +5.6%, financial regulation +12.0%, insurance +7.4% (medians); Dodd-Frank +23.2% (August 2010-July 2011 on the year before), the TCJA +24.7% (2018 on 2017), GDPR +18.9% (June 2018-May 2019 on the year before) (`tests/unit/test_pt_m12_drivers.py`).
6. **Wallets (sub-task 4).** Each sector's `mix` gains the ten practices ([A] from v2 s2 and s5: banks 0.30 in financial regulation, insurers 0.45 in insurance, energy companies 0.40 in energy and 0.10 in arbitration, pharma 0.40 in healthcare, technology 0.25 in technology and privacy, sponsors 0.12 in tax; normalised per practice as v1's), so real clients' wallets follow their sector and generated clients' their sector and size. **Individuals** (spec 7.1) are two pooled sectors (`private_wealth`, `claimants`, `pooled: true`) with one pooled client each per city (`clients.yaml pooled_clients`: "New York private clients", "New York claimants"; a city's made after its companies, so every other id is unchanged), mid-market, `real` (no generated failure), never drawn as an adverse party. Static panel firms gain the dossier's strengths (s8). Cities' `mix` gains the new practices (s7: London arbitration 2.5, insurance 2.0, mass torts 0.2; Houston energy 4.0; Washington antitrust, healthcare 2.5). Tax, financial regulation and healthcare join white collar as practices whose non-client adverse party is "the government". Tests: `tests/unit/test_pt_m12_wallets.py`.
7. **Rivals (sub-task 5).** Every rival personality's `mix` names the twenty ([A], v2 s8: elite lockstep firms about 22% in the new practices, the Big Four's legal arms 15% tax, litigation boutiques 8% arbitration and 5% insurance), and firms.yaml's `strong` gains the dossier's Chambers strengths (Cleary in antitrust, Davis Polk and Sullivan & Cromwell in financial regulation, Debevoise and Freshfields in arbitration, Covington in healthcare and privacy and 30 more). Three specialists join `firms.yaml` with new personalities, unpinned (fiscal 2005 [A]): Curtis, Mallet-Prevost (`arbitration_firm`), Withers Bergman (`private_client_firm`) and Lieff Cabraser (`plaintiff_firm`, San Francisco with a New York office; chosen over Milberg Weiss so that no real person carries the 2006-08 case: ADR PT-0013). Eighteen existing notables whose sourced 2005 role is in a v2 practice take it (Paulsson, Gaillard, Lamm, Rivkin and Sheppard to arbitration; Leddy, Hester, Kessler and five more to antitrust; Rosen and Morton to financial regulation; Czaban to healthcare; Voge and Warne to energy); no new real person is added. Arbitration, insurance and mass-tort spin-offs found litigation boutiques, and a rival's replacement key partners go to practices it has lawyers in. **T11** with the twenty (seeds 1-10, played to 2026): worst gap 5.1-7.2%, no miss, top 10 kept 8-10 (`tests/balance/test_pt_m5.py`); tests `tests/unit/test_pt_m12_rivals.py`.
8. **Contingency and funding (sub-task 6; ADR PT-0018).** A practice may carry its own billing-model mix (`practices.yaml billing_models`: mass torts contingency 0.90, private client retainer 0.45, arbitration success fees 0.25, financial regulation retainers 0.20; v1's ten keep `billing.models` by kind). A contingency case's WIP is off the ledger until the close (no 1100, 4000 or provision); its fee (25-40% of the recovery, PT-M3b's `contingency_bp`) is billed at standard 0 to 4100 from the recovery held in the client book. From January 2010 a funder offers each open contingency case terms (stream `funding` 120, keyed by the matter at its opening: `billing.funding.funded_bp` 50-75% of the WIP, `return_bp` 2-3x its advances, [A] v2 s6); `AcceptFunding` takes it: the WIP to date is advanced at once and each month's after (debit cash, credit **2800** funders' advances, non-recourse). At the close the funder is paid first from the fee (repaid advances clear 2800, the return above them is **6750** funding cost), capped at the fee; advances it does not recover are released to **4400** funded fees, so a funded loss costs the firm only the WIP it carried. PT-M3b's test of a contingency premium now expects the whole fee in 4100 (the brief's "no fee until its recovery" changed its premise). Tests: `tests/property/test_pt_m12_funding.py` (the waterfall over amounts; a played all-contingency firm taking every offer: 1100 and 2800 are billing's books and the client book's bank equals what it owes, every month; a forced funded loss and win).
9. **Entering a practice (sub-task 7; `ptk/sim/org/practices.py`).** A firm practises a practice when one of its lawyers has it as their home practice (role). `EnterPractice` (a player action) takes one of three routes: `lateral` opens PT-M4's lateral partner search in the practice (the firm practises it when the partner arrives); `team` brings `org.enter.team_partners` 2 equity partners with their books (a recruiter's fee and a year's guarantee each, their clients arriving by PT-M4's arrivals) and `org.enter.team_associates` 4 associates into the home office at once, by PT-M11's lateral-group builder (`geo.actions.build_team` gains a stream and a client-market argument; stream `practice_team` 121); `retrain` makes an equity or income partner's home practice the new one (energy `enter_practice` 10), their familiarity with it unchanged and growing only with hours (ADR PT-0006; a year full-time moves it a few points). A team or a retrained partner opens a practice group by PT-M2's rules (unlocked at 10 lawyers, one a practice); a lateral partner's group is the player's call. A six-lawyer team at once can sink a new firm (seed 1's spin-out dissolved within 18 months without a solvent balance): the team is a stage-2 move. Tests: `tests/unit/test_pt_m12_enter.py`; PT-M2's test that "tax" is an unknown practice now uses an unknown key (tax is a practice).
