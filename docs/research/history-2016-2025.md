## s2a. The pandemic's shape (Feb 2020 – Dec 2021)

**Law-firm demand (Thomson Reuters Peer Monitor).**
- Q1 2020: demand growth still positive overall (lawyers +1.8%, other fee earners +2.4% y/y) [S] (TR 2022 report, Fig. 5).
- March to August 2020: demand fell sharply. Q2 2020 demand was **−5.9% y/y**, the largest quarterly drop since 2009 [S] (Peer Monitor, 10 Aug 2020). Litigation −7.5% (court closures, postponements), corporate −5.5%, tax −9.1%, real estate −12.2%. Worked rates +5.2%, a record, partly from work shifting from associates to partners [S] https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2020/august/thomson-reuters-peer-monitor-index-falls-in-second-quarter.html . The TR 2022 report puts Q2 at about −6%, with other professional fee earners at −12.0% and lawyers at −4.9% [S].
- April–May 2020: demand fell an average 6.2% y/y and collection times slowed 3.4% (Rusanow). Wells Fargo put May 2020 demand "just over 10%" below May 2019. Clio (small firms, a different population) put billings −14% y/y in April and −27% in May [S] https://www.law360.com/articles/1285771 . **No Peer Monitor April-only y/y figure was found.**
- Q3 2020: lawyers −1.6%, others −8.2% [S]. Q4 2020: demand only −0.3% y/y. The Peer Monitor Index hit 69, its highest since 2006; PEP rose 11.5% in Q4 and Am Law 100 profitability rose 15% [S] https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2021/february/thomson-reuters-peer-monitor-index-hits-near-record-high-as-large-law-firms-show-resiliency.html . Full year 2020: **−1.6%** [S]. Am Law 100 FY2020 revenue +6.6% [S], with record profits.
- 2021: Q1 demand −1.0%, then Q2 **+7.3%** and Q3 the two strongest quarters of the decade (YTD Nov +4.0% vs 2020, +1.0% vs 2019) [S] (TR 2022 report; TR "Brief Interregnum" deck https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2020/12/A-Brief-Interregnum-Presentation.pdf). Q4 2021: +4.2% y/y (+3.8% vs Q4 2019), rates +3.6% [S] (TR Q4 2021 PMI). Full year 2021 **+3.7%** [S] (TR 2025 report). The surge was led by real estate and M&A/corporate, and was concentrated in the Am Law 100 (worked rates +5.6%) [S]. Collected realization reached 90.6%, the highest since 2009 [S]. Am Law 100 FY2021: every firm grew, RPL +12.5%, PEP +19.4% [S]; revenue about +15% [A].

### s4b. The 2021-2022 sequence, verified

3. **About 15-17 Jun 2021, Cravath and Paul Weiss:** matched Davis Polk. Law.com's headline reads "Cravath matches... offering $205K for first years" [S].
4. **29 Jun 2021, Milbank:** moved up to the Davis Polk scale [S].
5. **November 2021: no salary change.** November 2021 brought bonuses only. Cravath announced year-end bonuses on 22 Nov, and Davis Polk added special bonuses on 6 Dec [S].
6. **20 Jan 2022, Milbank:** **$215,000** for the class of 2021 [S].
7. **22 Feb 2022, Davis Polk:** matched $215k and paid more to senior classes, up to $396.5k [S].
8. **28 Feb 2022, Cravath:** $215k up to $415k, retroactive to 1 Jan 2022 [S].

### s4e. The spring 2020 cuts (brief)

- Bloomberg Law counted **at least 38 of the largest firms** that cut attorney or staff salaries, and at least 38 that cut or deferred equity-partner distributions, from February 2020. "Some large firms cut partner draws by 25-33%". Crowell & Moring, Hogan Lovells, Holland & Knight and Pillsbury cut draws by 25% [S, search summary]. Source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/law-firm-pay-cuts-to-continue-as-pandemic-worsens-in-u-s
- Associate cuts named in that coverage [S, search summary]:
  - Cadwalader: associates -25%, per a 31 Mar 2020 memo.
  - Squire Patton Boggs: associates -20%.
  - Davis Wright Tremaine: associates -12%.
  - BakerHostetler: associates and staff -10%, partners -15-20%.
  - Akerman: -7.5% for people earning $150k or more.
- **Boies Schiller:** no associate-cut figure was found. It was the only Am Law 100 firm to take a PPP loan, and it used the crisis to restructure. My memory [A] of a Boies Schiller salary cut is unconfirmed.
- Most cuts were reversed by the autumn of 2020, and Davis Polk led autumn special bonuses [S]. See also https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/law-firms-reverse-coronavirus-cuts-but-triage-not-over-yet
- **Suggested game reading:** a demand shock (lockdown) produces temporary cuts of 10-25% at weaker firms and draw cuts of 25-33%, with no cut at the Cravath-scale firms. The cuts reverse within 6-9 months, and once demand recovers, special bonuses follow.

**Pattern for the seeded macro:**
- Raises came when lateral and recruiting heat was high and top-firm profits had grown for 2-3 years.
- The first-year step was +8% to +16%: 2006 +16%, 2007 +10%, 2016 +12.5%, 2018 +5.6%, 2021 +7.9%, 2022 +4.9%, 2023 +4.7% [D].
- Senior steps were larger: 2016's 8th year +12.5%, 2022's 8th year +14% over July 2021 [D].
- Between raises came 1-9 years of flat base pay, with special bonuses as the pressure valve.

### s6 notes: read this first

1. **Five years are [S] for nearly every firm: FY2017, FY2019, FY2021, FY2024 and FY2025.** They come from five revisions of Wikipedia's "List of largest law firms by revenue", each a whole Am Law **Global 200** table for one year (source keys WG17, WG19, WG21, WG24, WG25). 4. **DLA Piper:** Global 200 and Am Law 100 figures are for the global verein. I give them for completeness. `firms.yaml` pins DLA on a US basis only to 2009, so do not join these to that path.
5. **`hogan_hartson`** is Hogan Lovells (global). From 1 July 2026 it is **Hogan Lovells Cadwalader** (Task B).
6. **`shearman`** stops at FY2023. From 1 May 2024 it is part of **A&O Shearman**, a UK-led combination with a firm the game does not model.

### s6d. Methods for the [A] cells

- **M1, single-year gaps (FY2016, FY2018, FY2020):** the geometric mean of the two [S] neighbours, `sqrt(V(y-1) × V(y+1))`. FY2016 uses `firms.yaml`'s FY2015 point as its lower neighbour, so it inherits that point's tag (revenue [S] G15; lawyers [D] M1). The method cannot show a dip or a spike. FY2020 (the COVID year: Am Law 100 +6.6%, ABA21) and FY2018 (+8%, L360-18) were both up years, so smooth interpolation is reasonable, but read each cell as ±5–8%.
- **M2, FY2022 and FY2023 when both are missing:** the firm's FY2021→FY2024 growth, split across the three years in proportion to the Am Law 100's aggregate log-growth: +2.7% in 2022 (ABA23 / ATL23), +6.8% in 2023 (LF24; Original Jurisdiction, https://davidlat.substack.com/p/2024-am-law-100-profits-partner-revenue-lawyer) and +13.3% in 2024 (search excerpt of Am Law 2025 coverage). The weights are FY2022 = 0.123 and FY2023 = 0.425 of the cumulative log-growth. Lawyers are interpolated geometrically by year.
- **M3, longer gaps** (Cadwalader FY2018–2020, Williams & Connolly FY2018–2021 and FY2024, Shearman FY2022, and lawyers wherever missing): geometric interpolation between the nearest [S]/[D] points on each side.
- **Not estimated:** Boies Schiller FY2025, Cadwalader FY2025 (Sept 2025 guidance was "more than $600M", BGOV-CWT25), and Shearman FY2024–2025 (merged).
